Six directions for the Sutra mark and app icons

Sutra (sūtra) means thread, the cord on which beads are strung, and also a terse rule, short statements strung into a discourse. It shares a root with sew, suture and seam. Every option below is drawn flat (no gradients, no shadows, no gloss), starts monochrome, and is generated by build.mjs. Scroll the table sideways on a phone.

Bead

Pearls on a thread

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Reads at once as a thing on a string, and the bead is the dye, so the colour system is the concept.
  • A home-screen row reads as a necklace: the thread runs edge to edge through every tile.
  • Cream tile, one dyed disc, one pictogram: calm, flat, and at home beside the paper style.
  • Flat discs with one pictogram stay readable down to 29 px.

Cons

  • The over-and-under crossing shows from about 64 px up; at 32 px and 16 px the mark is a ring with a bar, which can read as a diameter or no-entry sign.
  • Cream tiles have the weakest silhouette on a bright wallpaper; the thread helps but does not fix it.
  • Mark (ring) and icons (solid disc) are cousins, not the same drawing.
  • A ring on a line is not very ownable on its own.
Legibility at 16 px. Fair. The favicon stays a recognisable ring with a line at 16 px, but the crossing is gone, so it relies on the colour and the line to say bead.
OPTION 02

Loom

Warp and weft

Text and textile share a root: a discourse is a woven thing. The mark is a plain weave of four warp and four weft bars, each crossing resolved correctly over and under; every app icon is the same woven tile in its own dye, told apart by a different weave structure.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The most ownable shape: a woven square is a real structure, bold in one colour, and nothing like the old knot.
  • Icons are fabric swatches: eight weave structures in eight dyes make a beautiful sampler on the home screen.
  • The single-colour mark works through the gaps alone, so ink on paper looks right.
  • Every crossing is resolved by one rule in the generator, so over and under is correct everywhere.

Cons

  • Icons have no pictogram: apps are told apart by colour and weave, which has to be learned, and at 29 px the weaves blur into texture.
  • At 16 px the mark becomes a fine checkerboard; the favicon switches to a bolder 3 by 3 weave.
  • The busiest of the six; fine detail will sit awkwardly next to flat UI.
  • Basket and weave motifs are common in craft branding.
Legibility at 16 px. Weak. At 16 px the 4 by 4 mark is a checkerboard; the bolder 3 by 3 favicon holds as 'woven' but is not a distinct silhouette.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Needle, thread and running stitch say sew, suture and seam together, and the dashed hem is a friendly, ownable signature on every tile.
  • Pictograms stay plain and readable inside the hem, so apps are easy to tell apart.
  • The colour mark is warm and illustrative without a gradient.
  • Dashes are generated as a whole number, so corners and ends are always clean.

Cons

  • The needle is a thin diagonal: at 16 px it is a slash with a dot, so the favicon uses a heavier needle and drops the thread.
  • More illustration than icon; it is the most clip-art-like of the abstract set.
  • The hem turns into a dotted line at 29 px and is invisible below that.
  • At a glance the needle can read as a pen or a syringe.
Legibility at 16 px. Fair to weak. The needle silhouette is clear at 32 px; at 16 px only the slant and the eye remain, and the thread dashes are gone.
OPTION 04

Aphorism

Terse lines strung on one thread

A sutra is a short rule, and a discourse is many of them strung together. The mark is one unbroken thread running through four lines of different length: it reads as text, as a list and as a thread at once. Every app icon redraws its pictogram with the same lines and one dye-coloured line, on a kohl tile.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The logo and every icon are built from one element, a line, so the whole suite is one drawing language.
  • Kohl tiles with a single dyed line are sleek and make the dyes glow; every dye clears 3:1 on kohl.
  • The mark is a plain monoline: crisp at 32 px and up in one colour, and the favicon drops to three heavier lines.
  • It reads as text, a list and a thread at once, which is what a sutra is.

Cons

  • Eight near-black tiles make a heavy home screen, and the tile edge nearly vanishes on a dark wallpaper.
  • The mark can read as a stylised S, a 3, or a wind symbol.
  • Line pictograms overlap at 29 px (notes, journal and files are close cousins).
  • The dye appears only as one accent line, so colour identity is quieter than in the dye-tile options.
Legibility at 16 px. Fair to good. The four lines are about 1 px thick at 16 px and blur towards three bands in the plain mark; the favicon switches to three heavier lines on a tile and stays clean.
OPTION 05

Devanagari

The headline bar and the first letter of सूत्र

In Devanagari every word hangs from one unbroken headline, the shirorekha: a thread the letters are strung on. The mark is that bar with a geometric reduction of स (sa), the first letter of सूत्र, hanging beneath it; the app icons hang their pictograms from the same bar.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The most meaningful: the shirorekha is literally the thread that words are strung on, and the hanging pictograms extend the idea to every icon.
  • स reads clearly even at 16 px, the strongest small-size mark of the six.
  • Simple geometry: two kinds of stroke, one weight; icons carry a plain pictogram under a dyed bar.
  • Culturally grounded without any religious symbol: it is a letter of a living script.

Cons

  • Only readers of Devanagari get the reference; to everyone else it is an abstract glyph. It is an abstraction of स, not the exact letter, so a native reader should review the proportions before adoption.
  • Bar and hanger take about a third of each tile, so pictograms are smaller than in the other dye-tile sets.
  • A Devanagari glyph beside the Latin word needs careful lockup spacing.
  • The bar and hanger can read as a window title bar or a signpost on a first look.
Legibility at 16 px. Strong. A bar and one hanging letter, two kinds of stroke: legible at 16 px in one colour.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Understood instantly: a spool is the object, so there is nothing to explain.
  • Flat dyed tiles with large pictograms are the safest, clearest app icons (29 px is easy).
  • Lowest rollout cost: icons keep today's structure minus the gradient and gloss.
  • The single-colour mark keeps the flanges and the stripes, so ink on paper works.

Cons

  • Literal and a little generic: spools are common in sewing and craft brands.
  • At 16 px the mark is a striped I; the loose thread is lost.
  • The tiles carry no Sutra-specific device, only colour, so they are the least ownable.
  • A rigid tail drawn as a handle turns the spool into a mug, so the thread has to stay loose and flowing.
Legibility at 16 px. Fair. Flanges and stripes survive as an I-shaped reel and still read as a spool; the thread tail is lost, so the favicon omits it.
RECOMMENDATION

Recommendation

  1. 05 Devanagari. It is the only option whose idea is the meaning of the word: the headline bar is the thread, the letter hangs from it, and the icons hang their pictograms from the same bar. It is the strongest mark at 16 px (the favicon reads as a letter, not a smudge), it is one weight and two kinds of stroke, and the paper style suits it exactly (ink bar, ink letter). The icon system is distinctive but still plain: the pictogram stays large enough to read at 29 px. Condition: the glyph is an abstraction of स, not the exact letter, so ask a Devanagari reader to look at the proportions (box, diagonal, bar overhang) before it ships; the bar-and-hanger icon frame works even if the letter is later changed.
  2. 04 Aphorism. The safest strong choice: one line language for the whole suite, the cleanest monoline mark, every dye clears 3:1 on kohl, and the kohl tile is the paper style turned into an icon. Pick it over Devanagari if you want a mark that needs no explanation for anyone, at the cost of a darker home screen and a mark that can read as a stylised S.

Honourable mentions. 01 Bead has the loveliest icon row (the necklace) but the weakest mark. 03 Stitch and 02 Loom are the most characterful but need the most care at small sizes. 06 Spool is the lowest-risk, lowest-effort fallback.

How we would roll it out

  1. packages/ui/src/assets/brand.tsx: replace THREAD_PATH, THREAD_LOOP, THREAD_BEAD, THREAD_LOOK and THREAD_PAINT with the chosen glyph geometry (64 box, from build.mjs; change the viewBox to 0 0 64 64). Keep the SutraMark variant names and props: glyph is the mark in currentColor plus the accent; tile becomes a flat --primary tile with a --primary-foreground glyph (no gradient, no shine, no inner border); solid keeps following the paper style; outline and badge frame the new glyph. Delete the two linearGradient blocks and the ids they needed.
  2. SutraAppIcon: in sutraAppMeta replace the from / to gradient pair with one dye (and a soft tone for the bar or hem), swap appGlyphs for the shared 24-box pictograms in build.mjs, and drop the shine, the wave and the inner hairline. The shape prop (rounded, squircle, circle) stays.
  3. SutraWordmark: ship the drawn sutra path wordmark (wordmarkPath in build.mjs) beside the Geist text one, so the lockup no longer depends on a webfont, and retire the gradient prop. SutraLogo keeps its layouts and sizes.
  4. Tokens: data-app retunes the brand scale per app (--app-from / --app-to); point those at the eight dyes and set the accent for the paper style to ink as it is today.
  5. Favicon and app icons: copy the option's favicon.svg to apps/playground/public/favicon.svg and add a rel="icon" link in apps/playground/index.html; export 180, 192 and 512 px PNGs from app-icons/sutra.svg as full-bleed squares (the platform rounds the corners).
  6. Update the playground Assets page and any brand tests or snapshots (the API snapshot only changes if exports change), then add a short Brand section to the root README.md: the mark variants, the dye table, minimum size (16 px favicon, 24 px mark) and clear space (one stroke width).
